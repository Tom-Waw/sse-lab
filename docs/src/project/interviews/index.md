# Interviews

This section contains interview guides and generalized findings used for requirements engineering.

The interviews help ground the hospitality operations domain in realistic workflows, pain points, coordination problems, and information needs.

The goal is not to document a specific hotel, employer, vendor system, or proprietary process. Interview results must be generalized before they become requirements, issues, architecture decisions, or experiment ideas.

## Interview Approach

The initial interview process uses conversations with an experienced hospitality practitioner as generalized domain input.

Because the practitioner has worked across multiple hospitality areas, interviews are structured by operational perspective rather than by person. This helps keep each conversation focused and makes it easier to separate different stakeholder concerns.

The planned interview perspectives are:

1. Front office
2. Housekeeping
3. Night shift
4. Group reservations
5. Food and beverage / cross-department operations
6. Validation and prioritization

Findings from these interviews are practitioner-informed, not statistically representative. They are used to generate realistic project assumptions and requirement candidates, not to claim industry-wide validity.

## Confidentiality Rules

Interview material must remain generalized.

The repository must not include:

- hotel names;
- employer names;
- employee or guest names;
- screenshots of real systems;
- vendor-specific details;
- proprietary workflows;
- confidential operational information.

Interview results should be documented as generalized findings, not raw transcripts.

## Expected Outputs

Each interview should produce a small set of generalized findings.

A finding should describe:

- the generalized workflow or pain point;
- the stakeholder perspective;
- why it matters;
- possible relevance for scope, requirements, architecture, or experiments.

The interviews should not directly produce implementation tasks. Findings should first be evaluated against project vision, scope, stakeholder analysis, and the requirements engineering process.
